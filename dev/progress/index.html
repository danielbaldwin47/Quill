The page

Paper, margins, measure, scrolling, the empty state

Picked blind 2

Biggest gapNo page floor: A's last line's ink ends at y=1778 of 1800 (~11 CSS px of air below it) and the first line's ink starts at y=166 (~83 CSS px from the top edge) — the text field bleeds to the raw window edges with no bottom padding and no fade, so lines will scroll off a hard cut. B reserves a ~40 CSS px top zone and a ~50 CSS px bottom zone, and gives 114 px above the heading.

Paper is the same #F9F9F9, the column starts at x=672 in both, line pitch is 72 px (2x) in both, paragraph gap is exactly two lines in both — so this is decided entirely by how the field is bounded, and B bounds it badly. B spends a hundred pixels of vertical page on a filename you already know and a word count you did not ask for, then repays you by cutting a line of your own prose in half against a hairline. A's field is unframed and its last line sits eleven pixels off the sill, which is careless, but carelessness at the edge beats a running tally in your peripheral vision and a decapitated list item at every scroll position. Only one of these two pages lets you forget the application exists.

The type

Duo / Quattro / Mono, size, line height, rhythm

Still loses 4

Biggest gapMarkers render at #1C1C1C — the exact body ink, zero contrast, measured pixel-for-pixel against the paragraph grey — and the heading gets no weight or size step: 'The Lighthouse' is 2310 dark px in B vs 3350 in A on the same string at the same size, a ~45% ink deficit. '*Nothing out there is ever in a hurry,*' sets upright roman and '**bottle**' sets regular. Every structural cue in the file is invisible.

Round 4 · 28 Aug, 20:46

Cursor & caret

Shape, color, blink, motion, selection

Picked blind 1

Biggest gapA gives the selection two identical 4px caret bars, one at each end (device runs x426–433 and x734–741, both 8×92px, both #00B5FF at 100% alpha) — nothing distinguishes anchor from focus, so the caret stops meaning "where I am" and there is no cue which edge shift+arrow will move. B at least differentiates its two ends (handle above-left vs below-right).

The light-mode carets are effectively the same object in both — 8–9 device px (4 CSS px) of flat #00B5FF spanning the full 92px line box, a bar twice the width a text caret should be and tall enough to overshoot the ascender, so neither wins there and both read as a block, not a hairline instrument. The dark selection decides it: B hangs 22px touch-style dots off both ends of the range, which collide with the lines above and below and turn a keyboard selection into a phone gesture, and then dims and strikes the selected word down to 2.7:1 so you cannot read the thing you are about to replace. A's version is dumber but honest — the word stays at 7:1, nothing overlaps the neighbouring lines, and the range edge is the same 4px bar as the caret. A's real sin is that the two edge bars are identical, so the caret loses its one job of telling you where the next keystroke lands.

BuilderRebuilt the caret from re-measured iA pixels: #00b5ff, 0.155 em wide snapped to whole CSS px, height = full line pitch, and the band anchored 68.75% above / 31.25% below a runtime-measured baseline (iA sits lower than a CSS line box, centred on the ink rather than the em box), with the left edge nudged 0.07 em past the advance cell so it lands where the next glyph's ink begins; it rests on left/top rather than a transform so both edges are hard device pixels. The selection now paints in a new #sel-layer *under* the ink — the native textarea selection painted over #mirror and veiled selected text from #1c1c1c to #153d4d — snapped to the same band as the caret, with a caret-width bar bracketing each end (iA's touch handles minus the iOS knobs) and a short tail for a selected line break. Blink is a 1.06 s soft fade that switches off on every keystroke and caret move and returns after 480 ms of quiet; short moves glide 62/92 ms, long jumps and fast typing snap; unfocused, the caret stays as a quiet ghost and the highlight goes neutral. Typing latency unchanged (p50 2.44 ms to present, 100% within one 60 Hz frame).

Round 1 · 27 Aug, 01:36

Focus & typewriter

Sentence/paragraph dimming, typewriter line

Picked blind 1

Biggest gapThe caret — the one thing focus mode exists to point at — is soft and off-grid. A's caret measures 4x55 device px at 2x DPR but straddles a pixel boundary: columns 1842-1844 are solid #00b5ff, column 1845 is a partial at ~69% coverage, and the top/bottom rows are 63%/34% alpha, so it renders as a fuzzy ~1.75 CSS px sliver 27.5 CSS px tall (76% of the 36.4px line box). B's is exactly 6x70 device px of #00c3ff with zero partial pixels — 3 CSS px, 95% of the line box, hard-edged. Secondary but on the same wound: A leaves the '#'/'##' inline at x=643, so heading text starts 48px (2 chars) right of the body's left margin, while B hangs the markers (x=500 and x=476) and lands the heading text flush at 551 with the body.

A wins, but only because it actually understands what a sentence is: its dimming steps at the full stop, not at a line, so the sentence behind you and the sentence ahead stay at 4.9:1 while the page falls to 3.57:1 — B just slams everything that isn't the live sentence to one flat #707070 and lets you write into a void. The cost is that A's caret, the anchor the whole mode is built around, is a half-pixel-straddling 1.75px smear with 63%/34% alpha at its ends, next to B's crisp 3x35 CSS px bar; and A leaves its hashes inline so the headings sit 2 characters proud of the body margin that B keeps flush. Neither one is doing typewriter scrolling here — both are parked at the document top with the H1 clipped 2-4px from the window edge, and the active line lands at 49.9%/50.2% by accident of passage length, not by any lock. Both also dim the headings to the same #707070 as distant body text, so the moment you enter focus the document loses its skeleton.

BuilderDimming now reads theme.css's --fg-dim directly, so it is iA's measured #cccccc/#707070 exactly (dark was previously ~#434343 — far too dead). Added a third "near" tier for the sentence either side of the active one (+15% toward ink on light, +26% on dark) so the thought you are continuing stays legible without competing with the active line; paragraph mode stays deliberately binary. Sentence detection rewritten to survive abbreviations, initials, decimals, smart quotes, ellipses and "# 1." headings, and paragraph scope now breaks at headings/lists/quotes. Typewriter replaced with a retargetable rAF ease (120ms for a one-line advance, capped 280ms) that never fights manual scrolling, centres exactly at 0.50 on keyboard moves, and on a click only nudges to the nearest 18/82% band edge instead of throwing the page — the "screen jumping" iA's own docs warn about. Focus without typewriter keeps the active line inside a 15–85% band. Lines that change tier cross-fade in 130ms (one-shot, suppressed on load). Mod+D remembers the last sub-mode. Per-keystroke cost +0.3ms over focus-off (p50 1.6ms); verified in both themes, auto, and all three fonts.

Dark & light

Both palettes, contrast, switching

Picked blind 1

Biggest gapGlyph edges are soft: in the word "Down," A's edge pixels run 0.62 per core pixel across 205 distinct grey levels vs B's 0.55 across 23 — A's stems carry a visible halo where B's are cut clean, and it shows on the light ground at body size.

Both grounds are the same paper and the same ink (#f9f9f9/#1c1c1c, #1a1a1a/#cccccc), so this comes down entirely to the third tier — the grey between prose and background — and only A has one. In B the asterisks around *never* are exactly as black as the sentence they wrap, so every emphasis you type punches two extra full-weight glyphs into the line; A pushes them back to a mid grey (4.0:1 on paper, 4.6:1 on the dark ground) and the prose stays the loudest thing on the page. Worse, B's dark side draws a heading at #1c1c1c on #1a1a1a — a smudge you can see on an OLED at night and cannot read anywhere, and which light mode does not produce: that is a palette arrived at by inverting values, not by choosing them. A's own dark side is not designed either — the dim grey sits at the same 57% alpha in both themes and the caret is the same raw #00b5ff on white as on black, which on paper is the brightest object in the frame — but a slightly soft stem is a smaller tax than markdown syntax shouting in your face for the whole draft.

Markup rendering

Headings, emphasis, lists, quotes, code, links in the editor

Picked blind 5

Biggest gapNo blockquote rule at all: A draws a 4px #BFBFBF vertical rule at x=644 spanning the full 138px height of the quote, while B puts a lone '>' glyph on line 1 and leaves the gutter completely empty below it — B's second quote line sits at 674px with zero ink in the margin, typographically indistinguishable from body prose, so any quote longer than one line loses containment.

A indents your sentences by the width of its own syntax: type '## ' and the heading slides 105px right of the paragraph above it, and a blockquote that wraps drops its second line 54px left of its first. That is not markup rendering, it is markup occupying the page — you write down a fenceposted left edge that moves under you. B hangs every marker into the margin and never lets a glyph of prose start anywhere but 673px, which is the whole job; it pays for it with an empty gutter under wrapped quotes and markers at #7A7A7A instead of A's quieter #9E9E9E. A missing rule is a gap you can add in an afternoon; a text column that jumps is the design.

## #241 — wrapped markers

Four `mac-native-19-{light,dark}-wrapped-markers[-h6].png` frames use
[passage-wrapped.md](passage-wrapped.md). The `-h6` frames prefix exactly
`###### A heading at the widest gutter` and a blank line. Every item wraps to at least three
rows. The ordered marker is deliberately `123.` to distinguish its five cells from the

| Kind | Marker on body column? | Continuation anchor | Repeated marker? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `- ` | Yes | Body + 2 cells | No |
| `123. ` | Yes | Body + 5 cells | No |
| `> ` | Yes | Body + 2 cells | **No additional `>`** |

The default body advance starts at approximately x **673**, established by the 20-cell control
(selection starts at 672 and includes the selection edge). Bullet/quote continuation ink starts
at **726–729**; ordered continuation ink at **804–805**. These include each first glyph's inset:
the corresponding advance anchors are approximately 724 and 801 (2 × 25.6 and 5 × 25.6 past body).
In the H6 frame its first marker ink is x **495**, about seven cells left of the body.

Thus continuation rows stay under the item's **words**, with the marker's own width retained;
they do not return to the body's marker column. The H6 context does not change that rule. This
contradicts the interim continuation rule identified by #241; apply the design-row and Editor
change in its Linux half, followed by the Markup round.

| Conjunction | `#50812f` | `#8aa474` | `#43822d` | `#84a573` |

Each value is a repeated solid glyph colour, not an antialiased edge; the manifest gives counts
(964 or more pixels for each dark role, 990 or more for each light role). The light single-category
frames establish the mapping; dark uses the same words in the same TRIM passage. See the rig's
chromatic caveat before turning this table into engine constants.

**Dim wins over colour.** The second sentence's formerly coloured words become `#c7c4c2` on
light, `#707070` on dark. The bright first sentence retains its category colours.

**Token boundaries split words.** `I'll` leaves `I` plain and colours `'ll` as a verb. `can't`
colours `ca` as a verb and `n't` as an adverb. `it's` leaves `it` plain and colours `'s` as a verb.
`well-known` colours `well` as an adverb and `known` as a verb, leaving the hyphen plain.
`writer's` colours `writer` as a noun and leaves `'s` plain. A whole-word colour rule would lose
these distinctions. The engine palette, ORACLE test and syntax opponent remain downstream work;
this branch supplies their captured evidence and the colour-space qualification.

# Mac captures on the original rig — #419

The two narrower size classes at every text-size step, shot on the **built-in screen of the original
14-inch M1 MacBook Pro**, the display the committed captures were taken on.

[#344](CAPTURE-2026-09-10.md#344--the-window-narrows) found the three size classes and measured the
two narrower ones at steps 5 and 8 only. The wide-to-middle scale is 0.885 at one and 0.865 at the
other, so the twelve steps between could not be derived, and at 440 pt the container sat 32 px
inside what `window − 20 px` allowed with the gutter collapsed to a single cell — the narrowest
class's margins fell outside the container rule. [**#419**](https://github.com/danielbaldwin47/Quill/issues/419)
asked for both ladders in full and for what those margins follow.

**Both ladders are now measured, and the margins follow a rule.** Each class is fourteen sizes, the
same count as the wide class, five below Make Text Normal Size and eight above it. Neither narrow
ladder is the wide one scaled: the ratio wanders between 0.82 and 0.94 in the middle class and
between 0.70 and 0.83 in the narrowest, with no monotone trend. And the narrowest class's side
margin is **`max(5 pt, round(K − advance))`**, in points, with `K` **17.5 pt** in a window up to
390 pt and **22.5 pt** from 391 to 440 — a second break inside the class, at the window's width
alone, that no earlier capture saw.

All numbers are **device pixels** at backing scale 2, unless labelled pt. Regions are `[x,y,w,h]` in
logical points. [The manifest](capture-2026-09-13.json) names all 174 frames, their originals, both
hashes and each region; `rig/measure_419.py --verify` re-reads every number below off the frames.
The report changes no Quill geometry, colour, Template or Gate opponent; what the port does with it
is [#420](https://github.com/danielbaldwin47/Quill/issues/420)'s.

## Method

Per configuration: the passage plain, select-all for the container, and four selection fills on one
body row, with the advance fitted across the fills so that no side bearing and no selection edge
enters it. Two departures from #344's script, both forced by the range:

- **The fills are chosen per configuration.** A 40-cell run does not fit a row at step 13 of a
  400 pt window, and a 10-cell run is too short a lever at step 0 of a 960 pt one. The 4- and
  8-cell fills give a first advance, the container says how many cells the row holds, and two wider
  fills are shot inside that. Only `w0400-step13` ends with a two-point fit; eleven cells is all
  that row holds.
- **The body row is probed, not assumed.** The heading wraps to two or three rows in a narrow
  window, so the number of Down presses that lands on a body row is found per configuration.

**The driver dropped clicks twice, and both were caught by a control.** `rig/ladder_419.py` walks
the Text Size menu one click at a time and reads the pitch after each, which fixes each width's
ladder independently of the state runner. The first run reached each step out from Make Text Normal
Size and landed step 1 on step 2's size at both widths; the second walked up from the floor with
eight Smaller clicks, which is not enough to reach the floor from the top of a fourteen-step ladder,
and put steps 10 to 12 of the 400 pt run one and two rungs high. `rig/measure_419.py` now refuses to
print a table unless every frame's pitch sits on its width's rung.

**Fourteen sizes in each class, and the Default sits sixth in all three.** The continuous walk runs
out of ladder after five Smaller clicks and after eight Bigger ones at both 960 and 400 pt, which is
the wide class's count exactly. The floor at 960 pt is a 43 px pitch and the ceiling 150; at 400 pt,
37 and 129.

**Neither narrow ladder is the wide one scaled.** `scale vs wide` moves from 0.9195 to 0.8888 across
the middle class, dipping to 0.8211 at step 6, and from 0.7829 to 0.7790 across the narrowest,
dipping to 0.7003 at step 7. #344's two readings — 0.885 at step 5 and 0.865 at step 8 — sit inside
that wander and could not have predicted either end of it. Each class is its own ladder and has to
be carried as one.

**The liquid line height is each class's own, and it is the same falling curve.** `pitch / em` runs
1.61 → 1.35 in the middle class and 1.63 → 1.32 in the narrowest, against the wide class's
1.73 → 1.374. The narrower the class, the tighter the leading at the same rung.

## What the margins follow

The narrowest class was read at four widths across the whole ladder, the container's own left edge
off a select-all frame. 240 pt is the narrowest window the app allows: asked for 150, 180, 200 or
220, it returns 240.

The rule is written in points and the table in device pixels, so the two right-hand columns are the
rule doubled. It fits **all 56 rows**, at every width and every step.

**There is a second break inside the narrowest class, at 390/391 pt.** Bisected at three text sizes
— steps 0, 5 and 8 — it falls between the same two widths each time, so it is the window's width
alone, like the class breaks themselves. Below it `K` is 17.5 pt; from 391 to 440 pt it is 22.5 pt,
exactly 5 pt more. At 441 pt the middle class takes over and the margin is 10 px at every step.

**So the container rule holds everywhere once the margin is the class's own.** The container is
`min((limit + 14) cells, window − 2 × margin)`, where the margin is **5 pt** in the middle and the
wide class and `max(5 pt, round(K − advance))` in the narrowest. In the narrowest class the first
term never wins — 78 cells of even the smallest type wants 1062 px of an 800 px window — so the
whole class is the window term, which is why #344 saw a container 32 px inside what it expected: it
was reading a 5 pt margin where the app keeps 13.

**The container is centred to the half pixel.** Where the leftover is odd the extra device pixel
falls on the right: 20 px left against 22 right in a 480 px window at step 1, and 74 against 76 in a
1920 px one at step 5.

## What did not move

- **The middle class's container follows #344's rule unchanged.** At 960 pt the 78-cell term wins
  up to step 5 and the window term from step 6, and where the first wins the gutter is 6.88 … 7.01
  cells and the measure the whole 64.
- **Where the window wins, the gutter is not constant** — 5.95 cells at steps 6 to 8, then 4.99,
  3.96, 3.98, 2.99, 3.00. It is the leftover after a 10 px margin and a whole-cell measure, not a
  quantity the app holds.
- **Step 5 at 960 pt repeats #344 to three decimal places**: advance 22.653 here against 22.657
  there, container 1770 both times, pitch 63, gutter 6.97 against 7.02. The fits differ only in
  which fills they were taken over.
